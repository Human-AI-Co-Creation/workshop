About Workshop

Humans articulate visual creativity in early ideation through sketches, metaphors, and evolving expressions shaped by intuition, sensation, emotion, and experience, rather than through detailed, precise, and concrete visual or textual descriptions.

Recent advances in multimodal generative AI, including vision-language models and generative models, have dramatically expanded the capabilities of visual understanding and content creation. Commercial systems such as Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, Nano Banana, and Black Forest Labs demonstrate the potential of these systems to support artistic production and visual ideation at unprecedented scale.

Existing multimodal generative models are primarily optimized for semantic correctness in detailed and grounded multimodal understanding and generation. While effective, these objectives only partially reflect how humans naturally ideate and create: exploratory, divergent, intentionally imprecise, and especially fluid in early creative stages.

Rather than functioning as automated content producers, GenAI systems should act as collaborative partners that engage with humans to inspire and amplify creativity while preserving human agency and authorship. Designing multimodal GenAI that truly serves human creativity requires interdisciplinary work across model design, Human-AI interaction paradigms, and evaluation criteria that account for abstraction, subjectivity, and human-in-the-loop creation.

Call for Papers

Topics of Interest

Human-AI Co-Creation invites research on multimodal generative AI and human-AI collaborative creation for enhancing human creativity. The workshop focuses on GenAI as a collaborator, inspiration source, and assistant in open-ended, ambiguous, subjective, and iterative creative processes, while preserving human agency and authorship.

Human-centered GenAI models

Generative AI model design that is centered on human goals, control, intent, and creative experience.

Collaborative Multimodal AI

Multimodal AI systems that support human-AI co-creation across text, image, video, audio, 3D, and interaction signals.

GenAI for creative processes

Generative AI methods for creative workflows, design processes, artistic production, ideation, refinement, and iteration.

Multimodal representation and alignment

Representation learning and text-image, vision-language, or multimodal alignment for creative domains.

Creativity-oriented HCI paradigms

Human-computer interaction paradigms that support open-ended creative intent, exploration, feedback, and collaboration.

Cognitive foundations of creativity

Studies of human creativity and cognitive processes that can inform the design of generative AI systems.

Human-centric benchmarks and evaluation

Benchmarks and evaluation criteria for creative experience, preference, agency, authorship, and collaboration quality.

Novel applications for creativity

New GenAI applications in art, design, video, fashion, content creation, storytelling, and other creative scenarios.

Ethical GenAI in creative domains

Ethical questions around authorship, agency, copyright, attribution, creator rights, and responsible creative AI.

Invited speaker #3

Fabio Pellacini

Personal Page

University of Modena and Reggio Emilia

Fabio Pellacini is a Full Professor of Computer Science at the University of Modena and Reggio Emilia. His research focuses on the use of computer graphics methods to address design problems, with particular emphasis on creative, design, and entertainment industries. His work combines algorithms, efficient systems, numerical methods, and machine learning to support intuitive and interactive creation of complex 3D scenes, enabling both professional designers and novices to work with significantly less effort.

Before joining the University of Modena and Reggio Emilia, he was an Associate and Full Professor at Sapienza University of Rome, an Assistant and Associate Professor at Dartmouth College, a Visiting Assistant Professor at Cornell University, and a researcher in the R&D division of Pixar Animation Studios. He received his M.S. and Ph.D. in Computer Science from Cornell University and a Laurea degree in Physics from the University of Parma.

Pellacini has made significant contributions to appearance design, material and lighting editing, appearance fabrication, visualization of design workflows, and cloud-based collaborative design. He has received a National Science Foundation CAREER Award and an Alfred P. Sloan Fellowship, and regularly publishes in leading computer graphics venues.

Computer Graphics Visual Creation Interactive Systems
